- **Use FLUX + a better judge for now.** FLUX-schnell passed the strong referee (Qwen3-VL-235B) on 20/20 final drawings, against 6/20 for OmniGen2 (paired sign test p = 0.0001). FLUX was also recognizable to BioCLIP far more often (top-1 75% vs 30%), exportable as a trajectory every time (100% vs 60%), and about 1.8× faster per subject (113 s vs 202 s).
- **The production judge does not judge.** Local qwen2.5vl:7b passed 40/40 final drawings, including all 9 blank pages, which it described as "a simple cartoon doodle drawing of an animal". Its agreement with the strong referee is κ = 0.0. In production, only the OpenCV prescreen is actually rejecting anything. qwen3-vl-8b agrees with the 235B referee (κ = 0.83, 92.5% agreement), rejected every blank, and costs about $0.00004 per call on OpenRouter (or runs locally as qwen3-vl:8b).
- **OmniGen2 is faithful when it draws, but it often doesn't.** Its non-blank drawings follow the photo's actual pose and outline (silhouette IoU 0.78–0.91 on a lying cat, a coot, a betta and a frog), while FLUX draws a generic stock pose (median IoU about 0.2; a lying cat becomes a sitting cat). But 9/20 OmniGen2 final drawings are blank pages: whether it comes out blank depends on the seed, and three attempts were not enough to escape it. Others are filled black silhouettes (solid-fill ratio 0.42 vs 0.03).
- **The OpenCV prescreen is tuned for FLUX's line style.** It rejected some of OmniGen2's best drawings (betta IoU 0.89, frog IoU 0.91) for "too many components": OmniGen2's thin, broken sketch lines split into many pieces. If OmniGen2 is pursued, the prescreen needs a stroke-aware check rather than component counts.
- **Neither setup preserves the source viewpoint.** Facing-direction match is 65% for FLUX and 20% for OmniGen2 (mostly blanks). Angle-bucket match is near 0 for both, because the viewpoint classifier rarely gives a confident bucket for line drawings, so read the direction and symmetry deltas instead. Gold records currently carry the photo's angle label, which may not describe the drawing.
- **Memory is at the edge for both.** FLUX + Ollama was OOM-killed when both were resident; with FLUX unloaded between calls it completed, but swap was full (6.29 GB). OmniGen2 peaked at 20.9 GB RAM plus 5.9 GB swap. Neither setup has headroom on this 24.6 GB machine.
- **Robot cost is similar.** Estimated draw time is about 415–430 s for both. OmniGen2's drawings that it does produce are shorter (466 mm vs 821 mm of pen-down path), but its trajectories cover only 23% of its ink versus 63% for FLUX.
- **Next steps:** (1) switch the outline validator to qwen3-vl-8b; (2) lower FLUX strength, or condition it more strongly on the contour, so it keeps the source pose; (3) for OmniGen2, add a blank-output guard that resamples seeds before the judge, and loosen the component-count gates; then rerun r2 with the same suite.
